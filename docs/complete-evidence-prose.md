### Business explanation, verbatim

The report showed 8,765, matching the information feeding it. Earlier records totalled 7,661, placing the difference in the processing that prepares those records for the report. Matching records with other information can give one record several matches, causing its amount to be counted more than once. We have not confirmed which records caused the difference, whether the checks reflect the same moment, whether this behavior is intended, or the information before the last check. Recommended action: Ask the responsible business owner whether this behavior is intended; request a change if it is not.

### Technical explanation, verbatim

The measure returned 8,765 and the gold movement_values table also summed to 8,765, so the checked aggregate agrees across those two surfaces. The silver stock_movements_e1b8e1 table summed to 7,661. The declared transformation joins stock movements to rates on product_id with a left join before deriving movement_value, and the definition states matching rows may multiply because uniqueness is not assumed. That implemented join can carry units forward more than once and explains how the higher total can arise between silver and gold. This is observed aggregate agreement and a plausible mechanism, not proof that source stock movements are correct, that any inventory adjustment reflects business intent, or that the compared totals came from the same snapshot.

That row multiplication can increase the carried additive total of units, so a larger quantity in the upper table than in the lower table is directly accounted for by the join.
